Section 01
Early season design / ideas
August 2025 - November 2025

As a contrast to other years, we barely built over the summer. This was my first year taking robotics seriously, and Venom had previously won multiple awards in the past. During the break, we did find alot of useful ideas and videos online, all shared through the discord server. We had a very clear idea of how we thought the meta was gonna be and what we wanted to build. To us, it was obvious that the meta was gonna be a sort of basket robot that could hoard large amounts of balls. Our first design was admittedly horrible, it was super inconsistent with scoring and barely worked in general. It's not all negative, however, as the drivetrain we designed over the summer ended up relatively unchanged througout the season.

Section 02
Salish Secondary Tournament
November 8, 2026

Overall, this tournament was a major humbling and learning lesson for us. We ended up going 1-5 here, and it really had a big effect on morale and was just a major dissapointment. This tournament definately made us re-look our design, game and overal strategy, and we ended up completely rebuilding our robot after this. We realized that our robot design was horribly innefficient and slow, and had a huge amount of design flaws. All in all, it was a horrible, dissappointing tournament in terms of record and skills, but it for sure was an important, humbling lesson

Section 03
Heritage Woods Secondary Tournament
December 6, 2025

The team went 4-6 in qualifying and lost in the first playoff round, performing more dominantly than at Salish but falling short due to insufficient pre-playoff communication with potential alliances, which led to a poor pick and weak team chemistry. Technically, a new S-shaped scoring mechanism reduced jams and improved scoring accuracy, while flex wheels and rubber band sprockets boosted intake grip and cycle speed. Defensively, the team scouted opponents to target top threats, used their size to disrupt scoring, and leaned on a descore mechanism to pull balls from opposing goals.

Section 04
Southridge Tournament
January 17, 2026

Despite that strong finish, a coin flip between the #2 and #5 teams determined alliance selection and didn't go our way. The Round of 16 went smoothly with the team in full control, but quarterfinals slipped away in the final seconds when our entire tube got descored. The result was especially heartbreaking given how close we came to winning, having placed 4th in matches and 5th in skills overall.

Section 05
WPRA Scrimmage
January 30, 2026

The scrimmage was a valuable experience overall, offering strong practice matches and the chance to learn from top teams' strategies, practice routines, organization, and robot designs. The team stayed consistently in the top 8 through most of qualifiers, finishing 3-3, then comfortably won their first playoff round before falling to eventual champions 1010G and 10012Y; driving was arguably the team's best yet, and none of the losses were ones to be disappointed about. Autos ran smoothly overall despite the programmer's absence, with the lone failure coming in the quarterfinal round due to reliance on the weaker left-side auto, which had consistently underperformed in both matches and testing. In conclusion, the tournament was a great opportunity to compete against some of the region's best teams, learn from them, and meaningfully improve the robot.

Section 06
Surrey Christian Secondary Tournament
Febuary 7, 2026

This tournament was the team's best performance of the season, marked by a season-high skills score of 105 and a 5-1 qualifying record, with the lone loss in Q38 attributed to a motor disconnect and teammate errors. In playoffs, allied with 9568G, the team won the QF and R16 comfortably, edged out the #1-ranked alliance (winners of both design and excellence) by a single point in a tight semifinal, then lost a close finals match to the #2-ranked alliance. Skills runs were solid but limited: driver scores reached 63 with little room to improve given only two runs, while autonomous scored 42, falling short of the 69 goal due to a roughly inch-off misalignment on the final high goal despite the first two loaders clearing cleanly. The tournament also sharpened the team's strategic clarity heading into provincials, particularly around lower goal scoring, and culminated in an Innovate Award win for the hood bracket design and strong journaling, making this the season's most successful event with two trophies and clear gains in skills, driving, and strategy.

Section 07
Lower Mainland Regional / Provincials
Febuary 27, 2026

All in all, even though we didn't make worlds, provincials went great for us. After qualifications (we went 4-3), we placed around 12th, and got a good alliance from seaquam. Unfortunately, during playoffs, the opposing team ripped our battery out during the match, and even though we were easily winning at that point we ended up losing the match. We were hoping also to win a worlds-qualifying Innovate Award, and as the awards went on, we were getting more and more anxious. Even though we ended up not winning the award, it was a super close tournament and overall a great experience.

Section 08
End of season conclusion / thoughts
May 26, 2026

Honestly, as a whole, this was a great, super fun season of robotics. All the time that we spent working on the robot didn't even feel too exhausting, and I'm really happy to have joined Venom this year as it not only helped me learn tremendously in terms of Vex, it also gave me 4 great friends I could rely on througout the year. Despite not making worlds, the season for sure did not dissappoint, and I will for sure be taking robotics again next year, albeit with another team as all of my peers this year will be graduating.
