Mt Shuksan

Located in the North Cascades, Mt.Shuksan stands at an elevation of 2783m and prominence of 1344m

Trailhead → Base Camp September 19 | Departed 11:00 AM · Arrived camp 7:00 PMLeft the Shannon Ridge trailhead just before noon with full glacier kits — this late in the season you don't leave anything behind. The trail is entirely snow-free to the lower bivy area, which made for a warm, dry approach through the old-growth forest on the lower trail. The trail narrows and gets rougher through the blowdown section mid-route, but nothing that slowed us down seriously. GPS track pulled up a couple times through the less obvious sections. npsThe lower bivy area had plentiful running water, so we topped off bottles before continuing up to the upper bivy. The upper bench was where we made camp — mostly snow, a single flat dry spot already claimed by a prior party. We melted snow for dinner water since the upper bivy area may have water in the afternoon but is unlikely to in the morning — good to know for the pre-dawn start. The alpenglow hit the summit pyramid just as we finished setting up. Sleep came easy. npsnps📷 Photo 1 — Lower trail through old-growth forest. The quiet before the mountain. Dappled light, big trees, full packs. A grounding shot that earns the glacier views later.📷 Photo 2 — First view of the Sulphide Glacier and summit pyramid from the upper approach. The "reveal" moment. If you have one with the pyramid framed above the glacier bench, this is your section opener — wide enough to show the scale.Camp → Summit → Camp September 20 | Departed 7:00 AM · Summited 12:00 PM · Back at camp mid-afternoonUp well before light, out into cold still air. The glacier was hard and fast at that hour, crampons biting clean. There is much exposed rock and ice above the upper bivy area, and the route almost certainly requires travel on ice this late in September — we'd come prepared with tools and were glad for it. The boot pack from prior parties was still readable on the lower glacier, helpful in the pre-dawn dark. npsThe route crosses several open, visible crevasses before reaching the top of Hell's Highway, and after that travels through a very crevassed area using several snow bridges. We took our time here, probing bridges and moving one at a time across the sketchier crossings. Some parties have rerouted to climber's right to avoid this area — we could see evidence of an alternate boot pack doing exactly that, but the direct line held and we stuck with it. npsnpsAbove Hell's Highway the route transitions onto the summit pyramid — mixed rock and snow, the most committing section of the climb. No running water at the base of the summit pyramid, so we were working off what we'd melted at camp. The Center Gully was the crux, requiring careful footwork on steep terrain with real exposure below. Rockfall awareness matters here — many climbing parties have been ascending and descending the summit pyramid at the same time with reports of rockfall, so we moved efficiently and called out to parties above. npsnpsTopped out at noon. Baker to the south, the Pickets spread out to the east, the whole North Cascades in every direction. We sat longer than we should have.The descent of the pyramid demands full attention — easier to rush here than going up, and that's when mistakes happen. Back on the glacier the pace picked up and we were at camp by mid-afternoon.📷 Photo 3 — Pre-dawn headlamp shot on the lower glacier. Pure atmosphere — a cone of light on blue-grey ice, mountain invisible above. One of those shots that communicates the commitment of an alpine start without a word.📷 Photo 4 — Crevasse navigation on the upper glacier / Hell's Highway section. The technical crux of the route and visually the most dramatic glacier shot you can get here. Open crevasses, exposed ice, a rope team moving carefully — this tells the story of what late-season Sulphide actually demands.📷 Photo 5 — On the summit pyramid, looking back down the route. The exposure shot. Glacier sweeping down below, Baker and the Pickets beyond. Small figure on rock against an enormous backdrop beats a close portrait every time.📷 Photo 6 — Summit. The payoff. Keep it simple — person on top, clear sky, the world below.Camp → Car September 20 | Departed mid-afternoon · Arrived trailhead 11:00 PMBreaking camp on post-summit legs has its own particular character.
