Project 01

Weather Station

Temperature, pressure, humidity, elevation, heat index, and other environmental data in one compact build.

Open budget →+
Section 01
Project overview
May 25, 2026

In this project, my goal was to create a small weather sensor that could detect temperature, humidity, elevation and etcetera. To do this, I used an Arduino-based microcontroller, along with simple electronics parts like a breadboard and wires. The sensors were also chosed to work with the Arduino, and also picked up a small OLED display to display readings. I selected an Arduino-based ecosystem to simplify the programming and software. Another main goal was to create a program or app that could connect to the station, and record data over a long time.

Section 02
Electronics
May 30, 2026

In this build, I used 4 main components: a DHT-22 temperature/humidity sensor, a ESP-32 microcontroller, a 1.3 inch I2C OLED display, and a BMP-280 pressure/temperature sensor. All electronics ran at 3.3V, and were powered by a solar-powered micro-USB power source. All parts came pre-soldered, except for the OLED display which I did myself. Also, it's worth noting that I chose the ESP 32 specifically for it's wireless capabbilities.

Section 03
Enclosure and layout
June 3, 2026

For organization, I used a breadboard. This allowed me to keep all the loose wires together, and to simplify the shape/design for an enclosure. I am currently in the process of making said enclosure, but for now I've taken measurements and made a rough prototype using OnShape, will most likely be 3d printed. Unfortunately, I did not have the chance to crimp custom-length wires, and as a result was forced to use wires that were too long.

Section 04
Programming
June 6, 2026

First off, as mentioned before, I used Arduino IDE. I chose this because it mainly uses cpp, and because of it's simplicity. Cpp is probably my best language, and it was for sure a benefit when working with an unfamiliar IDE. There were multiple libraries I had to download, mainly just ones for the sensors and their dependancies. The programming was relatively easy, and I also used Claude Code (Genuine lifesaver) for debugging whenever needed.

Section 05
Calibration and testing
June 9, 2026

To start, calibration was extremely easy, as it seemed that all of the sensors came pre-calibrated. The only thing I had to calibrate on my own was the elevation, and besides that nothing was really too big of an issue. Testing wise, it was pretty crude but I used a piece of ice, a humidifier, a lighter and even drove up my hill, and all went well. The only caveat was that I ended up having to redo my wiring, as alot of the pins on mine were bending and disconnecting, and had to melt down and redo the soldering for the same reason. Attached is a testing video with ice, you can clearly see the temperature readings lowering as it refreshes.

Section 06
Future steps and final thoughts
June 11, 2026

Overall, I would say that this project was a resounding success, especially for the such little time I had. A lot was learnt during this project, namely better soldering skills, experience with microcontroller programming, and most importantly in my opinion the use of AI in debugging and testing. Moving forward, I would like to first 3d print a suitable enclosure for it, fully redo the wiring and hopefully be able to crimp my own for perfect sizing, and finally write new code so it could use WiFi to send data over onto an app or device to record data long-term.
